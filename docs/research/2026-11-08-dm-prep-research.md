# DM Improv & Prep Suite: Research Brief

**Date:** 2026-11-08
**Status:** Pre-design research. Nothing is decided yet. This feeds the design questionnaire.

---

## 1. The problem, in DMs' own words

Across forums, blogs and the big prep-advice authors, the same pains keep coming up:

| Pain | What it looks like at the table |
|---|---|
| **Name freeze** | "When pushed, I panicked and said a monster's name was Waangkor." Sly Flourish polled DMs, and hundreds named *a good list of names* as their #1 improv aid. |
| **Name *recall*** | Often a bigger problem than inventing names: "it's remembering it later, even within the same session." DMs keep paper lists and cross names off as they use them. |
| **Names that blur together** | Players can't tell NPCs apart because the names sound alike. Generators that output "too similar" names make this worse. |
| **Over-prep** | Hours spent on plots the players skip. This is the core worry behind "Don't prep plots, prep situations" (The Alexandrian) and the whole Lazy DM movement. |
| **The stall** | Players miss the one clue, or ask a question the DM didn't anticipate, and the game grinds to a halt. |
| **Disconnected tools** | Name gen in one tab, tavern gen in another, notes in a third. Nothing links up. Even vendor roundups now say the winning tools *share context*: the NPC you generate should live in the town you generated. |
| **Generic slop** | Random generators (and especially AI) produce bland or contradictory output that breaks the established lore. |

**The gap we can own:** an *integrated* improv kit that's (a) instant at the table, (b) context-aware, (c) remembers what you actually used, and (d) is built around proven prep methods instead of being a bag of disconnected generators.

## 2. The canon of top DM practices (what to bake in)

### 2.1 Prep philosophies

**Sly Flourish: *Return of the Lazy Dungeon Master* (eight steps; renamed to "Lazy GM" in the 2026 *Rise of the Lazy Gamemaster*)**
1. Review the characters
2. Create a strong start
3. Outline potential scenes
4. Define secrets & clues (~10, 1–2 sentences each, *not tied to where they're found*)
5. Develop fantastic locations (evocative name + 3 aspects)
6. Outline important NPCs (name, archetype, a mannerism or connection)
7. Choose relevant monsters
8. Select rewards
→ *Implication: a natural backbone for a "Session Prep" workspace. It's system-agnostic by design and the most widely adopted modern prep method.*

**Justin Alexander: The Alexandrian**
- **Don't prep plots, prep situations.** Plots have chokepoints; situations have pressure.
- **Three Clue Rule:** for any conclusion you need the PCs to reach, plant at least 3 clues.
- **Node-based scenario design:** invert the rule. If PCs can reach any 3 clues, they'll reach at least 1 conclusion. Nodes = locations/people/events linked by clues.
- **Prep toolkit thinking:** reusable "tools" (an NPC roster, an encounter table, a rumor table) instead of linear scripts.
- **Random encounters as world-building:** the encounter table is a statement about the region.
→ *Implication: clues are first-class objects that link to nodes; a "clue web" view; warning when a conclusion has fewer than 3 clues.*

**Phil Vecchione: *Never Unprepared***
- Prep has phases: **Brainstorm → Selection → Conceptualization → Documentation → Review**.
- Know your prep "type" and your time budget.
→ *Implication: a quick "brainstorm dump" inbox that turns into structured cards.*

**Powered by the Apocalypse / Dungeon World**
- **Fronts** with **Grim Portents** (escalating steps toward an Impending Doom) and **Stakes questions**.
- **GM Principles:** "Draw maps, leave blanks", "Ask questions and use the answers", "Be a fan of the characters", "Think offscreen, too".
- **GM Moves:** a menu of things to do when players look to you (reveal an unwelcome truth, show signs of an approaching threat, put someone in a spot, offer an opportunity with a cost, and so on).
→ *Implication: a "Move Deck" for when you're stuck; fronts as a threat tracker.*

**Blades in the Dark (John Harper)**
- **Progress clocks** (4/6/8 segments) for threats, projects and faction plans.
- **Faction game**: factions advance between sessions whether or not the players engage.
→ *Implication: clocks as a universal UI widget; a "between sessions, the world moves" faction turn.*

**Kevin Crawford: *Stars/Worlds Without Number*, *Sine Nomine* toolkits**
- **Tag system:** a place gets 2 tags (e.g., "Theocracy", "Cursed Ruins"), and each tag supplies Enemies, Friends, Complications, Things and Places. Very high improv yield per word.
- **Faction turns** and "adventure creation from conflicts."
- Free SRD-style tables with deep reuse.
→ *Implication: tags are an excellent generation primitive. Pick two tags and get an instant adventure seed.*

**Johnn Four: Five Room Dungeon**
- Entrance/Guardian → Puzzle/Roleplay → Trick/Setback → Climax → Reward/Twist.
→ *Implication: a one-click adventure-site template.*

**The Angry GM**
- Every encounter needs a **dramatic question** and a goal beyond "kill everything."
- Tension and pacing as a managed resource.
→ *Implication: encounter cards include "What's the question? What happens if they fail?"*

**Matt Colville: *Running the Game***
- Start small (a village, a dungeon), grow outward. Begin in the action. Villains have plans and act on them.

**OSR practices**
- **Overloaded encounter die** (Necropraxis): one roll gives encounter / sign of passing / environmental shift / depletion / and so on.
- Rumor tables (some true, some false), reaction rolls, morale.
- Hex/pointcrawl procedures; "the dungeon as an ecosystem."
→ *Implication: system-neutral procedure widgets (reaction roll, morale, overloaded die).*

### 2.2 Improvisation techniques

**Mythic GME (2e)**
- Yes/No oracle weighted by likelihood (and "chaos factor").
- **Meaning Tables**: random *verb + subject* word pairs ("Betray / Wealth") for interpretation.
- Random events that interrupt scenes.
→ *Implication: an Oracle panel. Ask a question and get yes/no/and/but plus a meaning prompt.*

**Ironsworn / Starforged Oracles**
- Thematic oracle tables ("Action + Theme", "Descriptor + Focus") that replace or assist the GM.
- Licensed openly (CC-BY for Ironsworn), so they're a usable reference for structure.

**Improv-theatre transfers**
- "Yes, and" / "Yes, but" / "No, but". Name it first, detail later. Give every NPC *one* memorable hook, not a biography.
- **NPC triad:** a *want*, a *fear or secret*, a *tell* (voice, gesture, look).

**Other tools DMs swear by**
- Tarot and playing-card "spreads" for plot inspiration; Rory's Story Cubes.
- Pre-written "stuff in pockets": 3 random trinkets, a random overheard conversation, a weather report.
- **The names list**: the single most-cited improv aid.

### 2.3 Session running and memory
- **Session log**, kept live: who they met, what they learned, what they promised.
- **Recap prompts** at the start of the next session.
- **Safety tools:** session zero, lines & veils, X-card, Script Change, Stars & Wishes.

## 3. Competitive landscape

| Tool | What it does well | Where it falls short (our opening) |
|---|---|---|
| **donjon** | Fast classic generators (dungeons, names, towns); free; a staple for 10+ years | Dated UI; nothing connects; no memory |
| **Kassoon** | Broad free 5e generators | 5e-locked; disconnected |
| **Perchance / Chartopia** | Thousands of user-built tables; generator *builders* | Not TTRPG-focused; quality varies wildly; no campaign context |
| **Fantasy Name Generators / Roll For Fantasy** | Very deep name libraries | Names only; no "used names" log |
| **Kobold+ Fight Club** | Encounter balancing | 5e combat only |
| **World Anvil / LegendKeeper / Kanka / Obsidian** | Deep wikis and worldbuilding | Heavy prep tools, slow at the table, no generation (or bolt-on AI) |
| **AI DMs (Friends & Fables, AI Dungeon, Voyage)** | Replace the GM | Not what a human DM needs; long-campaign drift |
| **AI prep assistants (Archivist, CharGen, Quest Portal, Keeper of the Realms)** | Session notes, NPC/token/map generation | Mostly 5e-leaning; output can contradict lore; usually subscription |
| **Mythic GME apps** | Oracle play | Solo-focused; no prep layer |

**Positioning:** *"The improv kit behind your screen."* It's not a wiki and not an AI GM. It's the fastest way to never be stuck, which keeps the human DM in charge while their prep accumulates into a living campaign codex.

## 4. Emerging product shape (a hypothesis, to be confirmed by the questions)

Three modes on one shared data model:

```
              ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
              │                CAMPAIGN CODEX                │
              │  NPCs · Places · Factions · Clues · Items    │
              │  Clocks · Session logs · Used-names ledger   │
              └───────▲──────────────▲──────────────▲────────┘
                      │              │              │
   ┌──────────────────┴──┐  ┌────────┴─────────┐  ┌─┴────────────────────┐
   │   PREP DESK         │  │   TABLE MODE     │  │   GENERATOR FORGE    │
   │ (between sessions)  │  │ (live, 1-tap)    │  │ (all generators,     │
   │ 8-step session prep │  │ Improv kit sheet │  │  lock & reroll,      │
   │ Fronts & clocks     │  │ Oracle + Moves   │  │  chain into codex,   │
   │ Clue web (3-clue)   │  │ "Name now" + log │  │  custom tables)      │
   │ Faction turn        │  │ Live session log │  │                      │
   └─────────────────────┘  └──────────────────┘  └──────────────────────┘
```

**Key design ideas from the research:**
1. **Pre-rolled Improv Kit:** before each session, auto-generate a one-screen/one-page sheet: 20 names (sorted by culture), 6 instant NPCs, 4 locations, 6 rumors, 6 complications, trinkets, weather, a few Meaning pairs. *Prep already done.*
2. **Use = Log:** tap a name or NPC at the table and it's stamped into the session log and codex. That solves the recall problem.
3. **Context chaining:** generating a tavern also offers its keeper, a regular, a rumor and a menu, all pre-linked.
4. **Lock & reroll:** keep the parts you like and reroll the rest.
5. **Distinctiveness guard:** the name generator avoids starting letters and sounds already in the campaign.
6. **Tags as the generation primitive** (Crawford-style): two tags → enemies, friends, complications, things, places.
7. **Stuck button:** one tap gives a GM move + a Meaning pair + a complication.
8. **Clocks everywhere:** threats, factions, projects.

## 5. Starter templates (content models)

These are the "shapes" every generator and prep card would fill. System-agnostic unless noted.

### 5.1 Session Prep (Lazy DM 8 steps, extended)
```
SESSION  #__  · Date ____ · Campaign ____
1. CHARACTERS   — For each PC: spotlight hook this session / open thread
2. STRONG START — Scene that opens in motion (1–3 sentences)
3. SCENES       — 3–5 *potential* scenes (bullet, not script)
4. SECRETS & CLUES — 10 items, 1–2 sentences, unattached to location
                 [ ] revealed?  → links to: conclusion / NPC / place
5. LOCATIONS    — Name + 3 evocative aspects (sight / sound / smell / oddity)
6. NPCs         — Name · archetype · want · tell
7. THREATS      — Monsters/opponents + their goal in this scene
8. REWARDS      — Treasure, info, allies, leverage
+ FRONT CHECK   — Advance which clocks? What happened offscreen?
+ SAFETY        — Lines & veils reminders for this content
```

### 5.2 NPC Card ("one-breath NPC")
```
NAME          (pronunciation)         CULTURE / ANCESTRY
ROLE          e.g., ferryman, debt collector
LOOK          one striking detail
TELL          voice / gesture / catchphrase
WANT          what they're after right now
FEAR/SECRET   what they're hiding or dreading
LEVERAGE      what PCs can offer or threaten
RELATION      → links to faction / place / other NPC
[stat line slot — optional, system pack]
FIRST SEEN    session # (auto-logged)
```

### 5.3 Location Card
```
NAME              "The Drowned Lantern"
TYPE              tavern / ruin / district / wilderness node
3 ASPECTS         sight · sound · smell (or oddity)
WHO'S HERE        2–3 NPC links
WHAT'S HAPPENING  a current tension or event
HIDDEN            a secret / clue placed here (optional)
EXITS / LINKS     → other locations (node graph)
```

### 5.4 Secret / Clue Card
```
SECRET     one or two sentences of truth
POINTS TO  conclusion / NPC / place / front
DELIVERY IDEAS  (suggested, not fixed): overheard, document, NPC slip, physical trace
STATUS     hidden · hinted · revealed (session #)
```
The clue web warns when a conclusion has fewer than 3 clues.

### 5.5 Front / Threat (PbtA + clocks)
```
FRONT          The Ash Synod
IMPENDING DOOM what happens if no one intervenes
GRIM PORTENTS  ① ② ③ ④ (escalating steps) — as a 4/6/8 clock
STAKES Qs      "Will Brother Ilen survive the purge?"
CAST           linked NPCs, places
```

### 5.6 Faction
```
NAME · GOAL · METHOD · RESOURCES · LEADER · RIVAL · CLOCK
Between-sessions turn: roll/choose advance → write consequence → log
```

### 5.7 Encounter (Angry GM-style)
```
DRAMATIC QUESTION  "Can they cross the bridge before it burns?"
OPPOSITION         who/what + their goal
TERRAIN/TWIST      one feature, one complication at round 3
FAIL FORWARD       what happens if they lose / flee
[system pack slot: stat blocks / difficulty]
```

### 5.8 Five Room Dungeon
```
1 ENTRANCE / GUARDIAN · 2 PUZZLE / ROLEPLAY · 3 TRICK / SETBACK
4 CLIMAX / BIG BAD · 5 REWARD / REVELATION / TWIST
```

### 5.9 Improv Kit (the one-pager)
```
┌ NAMES (20, grouped by culture) ┬ INSTANT NPCs (6 one-breath) ┐
├ PLACES (4 w/ 3 aspects)        ┼ RUMORS (6; T/F marked)      ┤
├ COMPLICATIONS (6)              ┼ TRINKETS / LOOT (6)         ┤
├ MEANING PAIRS (6)              ┼ WEATHER / OMEN / SOUNDS     ┤
└ GM MOVES reminder strip        ┴ ORACLE odds strip           ┘
```

### 5.10 Session Log
```
Session # · Date · Players present
Timeline (auto-stamped): ▸ met [NPC] ▸ learned [Clue] ▸ visited [Place]
Promises & debts · Loose threads · Recap for next time (auto-drafted)
```

## 6. Generator catalogue (candidate modules)

**People:** names (per culture, per genre) · one-breath NPCs · villains with plans · crowds/patrons · voices & mannerisms · relationships
**Places:** settlements · taverns/inns · shops & wares · districts · dungeons (5-room) · wilderness nodes · landmarks · weather/season
**Plot:** adventure seeds (tag pairs) · secrets & clues · rumors · complications · twists · hooks per PC · quest givers
**Things:** trinkets · treasure · magic items (system-neutral descriptions) · books/letters/handouts · signs & graffiti
**Factions & threats:** factions · fronts · cults · monster motivations · encounter tables by region
**Improv aids:** Oracle (yes/no/and/but) · Meaning pairs · GM moves deck · "stuck" button · random event · reaction roll · overloaded encounter die
**Flavor:** tavern menus · overheard conversations · street scenes · smells/sounds · insults & oaths · songs/ballad titles

## 7. Licensing notes (for content we ship)
- **D&D SRD 5.2** is released under **CC-BY-4.0**, so 5e mechanics and monsters are usable with attribution in an optional system pack.
- **Ironsworn** core content is CC-BY. **Mythic GME** content is *not* freely licensed (its digital tools reference CC BY-NC for some uses), so we should design an original oracle rather than copy its tables.
- Sly Flourish, Alexandrian, Crawford and others are *methods*, which we can implement freely. We shouldn't copy their table text verbatim; we write original content inspired by the structure.
